A partnership approach

Not just funding.
Long-term collaboration.

Strong partnerships begin with a shared understanding of the problem—and a clear agreement about what will be done, how progress will be monitored and how learning will be shared.

The opportunities below are suggested pathways for discussion, not pre-approved funding packages. Scope, budget, geography, duration and measurable outcomes should be agreed jointly and supported by a documented project plan.

Ways to collaborate

Purpose-led pathways for stakeholders.

01
CSR & philanthropy

Support a defined programme

Explore support for river restoration work, adult literacy, community institutions, climate resilience, livelihoods, health awareness or rights facilitation.

  • Agreed target communities and activities
  • Time-bound work plan and budget
  • Monitoring, learning and reporting approach
Discuss programme support →
02
Universities & research

Build evidence together

Collaborate on field research, community documentation, policy engagement, water-quality monitoring, ecological flow and social inclusion.

  • Ethical, participatory research design
  • Clear data-use and consent protocols
  • Outputs useful to communities and practitioners
Discuss research collaboration →
03
Civil society & networks

Connect people and institutions

Work across campaigns, share methods, convene discussions and strengthen collective advocacy on rivers, forests and community rights.

  • Shared learning and peer exchange
  • Coordination around defined issues
  • Community voice in public conversations
Explore collaboration →
04
Technical & in-kind support

Bring capability to the work

Offer specialist knowledge, accessible technology, monitoring tools, training resources, communication support or professional volunteering.

  • Specific, practical contribution
  • Responsible data and safeguarding practices
  • Skills transfer where appropriate
Offer expertise →
