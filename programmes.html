Intervention areas

Five themes.
A whole-community lens.

The areas below reflect CAR’s supplied organisational profile. River restoration and Forest Rights Act facilitation are cross-cutting strands highlighted in the recent activity report.

01
Livelihoods & resilience

Sustainable livelihoods

Stronger livelihoods reduce household vulnerability and help communities rely on locally rooted skills, resources and institutions. CAR’s work includes agriculture and horticulture support, livelihood diversification, women’s economic empowerment and small-scale entrepreneurship.

Activities in the profile

  • Sustainable agriculture and horticulture support
  • Community-based seed banks and grain banks
  • Skill development and livelihood diversification
  • Forest-based livelihood opportunities
  • Access to government livelihood programmes
  • Strengthening community economic institutions
INTENDED DIRECTIONGreater self-reliance

More resilient households, stronger local institutions and dignified livelihood options.

02
Health, nutrition & sanitation

Healthier, informed communities

CAR promotes awareness and improved access to essential health and nutrition services among tribal communities and vulnerable households, with particular attention to women, adolescent girls and children.

Areas of focus

  • Preventive health awareness and maternal/child health
  • Nutrition awareness
  • Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH)
  • Community health awareness and referral
  • Linkages with public health services
  • Promotion of healthy and safe practices
INTENDED DIRECTIONHealth with dignity

Stronger awareness, safer practices and better connection with essential services.

03
Education & skills

Learning at every age

CAR works to improve educational opportunity for children and young people in underserved tribal communities while promoting adult and functional literacy. The emphasis is on practical learning that strengthens confidence and participation in everyday life.

Areas of focus

  • School enrolment and retention
  • Children’s learning support and parental awareness
  • Adult and functional literacy
  • Educational support for vulnerable children
  • Youth development and vocational skills
  • Digital and life-skills awareness
INTENDED DIRECTIONLearning and agency

More opportunities to read, write, participate, make informed choices and realise potential.

04
Environment & climate resilience

Healthy ecosystems, resilient communities

CAR integrates environmental sustainability into development work because the lives and livelihoods of tribal communities are connected with land, forests, water and common resources. Activities include awareness, regeneration and local action on resource protection.

Areas of focus

  • Climate-change and environmental awareness
  • Natural-resource management
  • Forest and biodiversity conservation
  • Water, soil and land conservation
  • Traditional agriculture practices
  • Forest regeneration through seed balls
  • Environmentally responsible livelihoods
INTENDED DIRECTIONEcological resilience

Healthy ecosystems that support water security, livelihoods and future generations.

05
Social justice & rights

Rights, voice and participation

CAR facilitates awareness of legal rights, access to government schemes, social protection and constitutional entitlements for vulnerable communities. Recent highlights include Forest Rights Act facilitation and engagement on tribal identity and rights.

Areas of focus

  • Forest Rights Act awareness and facilitation
  • Linkages to government support schemes
  • Interaction with local government representatives
  • Women’s participation in governance
  • Community leadership, knowledge and skills
  • Advocacy on issues affecting communities
INTENDED DIRECTIONJustice and voice

Communities better equipped to understand their rights, engage with institutions and participate in public decisions.

Cross-cutting focus

Restoring rivers.
Defending water justice.

CAR’s recent field and advocacy work highlights the Baitarani and Salandi rivers, including concerns about encroachment, sand mining, pollution, changing flows and water stress affecting farmers. The organisation has supported public meetings and community mobilisation for river protection.

What comes next

From local practice
to shared learning.

CAR’s stated future priorities include community-based restoration, stronger rights facilitation, research partnerships, knowledge-sharing tools and social inclusion.

01

Scale river restoration pilots

Develop community monitoring frameworks alongside restoration work on small rivers.

02

Strengthen rights facilitation

Build Forest Rights Act knowledge, legal-aid linkages and capacity in tribal districts.

03

Grow institutional partnerships

Develop long-term collaboration with CSR programmes and academic institutions.

04

Create a knowledge repository

05

Support citizen science

Develop practical tools for water-quality and ecological-flow monitoring.

06

Strengthen gender and social inclusion throughout programme design and delivery.
